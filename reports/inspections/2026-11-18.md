- **`no_slot`** (`apps/backend/src/fleet/claim.ts`, the `no_slot` answer in
  the claim handler): no free fleet slot for the worker. Tickets minted:
  `worker-60-14-3` 237, `homelab-8` 237, `homelab-5` 236, roughly one every
  six minutes each, none claimed. The worker exits before opening a browser,
  so these are not Facebook logins.
- **Fleet `422`s are slow bodies, not bad payloads.** All seven took
  11.7–113.6 s to arrive (session PUTs 11.7, 43.4, 39.3, 14.8, 21.9 s;
  heartbeat 26.1 s; `worker-60-14` report 113.6 s), where the same routes
  normally answer in tens of ms. The handlers parse with
  `c.req.json().catch(() => null)` and answer `422` when the body is missing
  or cut off (`apps/backend/src/fleet/hosts.ts`, `apps/backend/src/fleet/claim.ts`).
  Same-sized requests from the same hosts succeed the rest of the day, and
  the next cycle succeeded each time. This is the same transport pattern as
  11-17's report, now also seen once from `worker-60-14`, not only homelab.
- **Parked workers.** At 07:27 `homelab-1` and `worker-60-14-1` parked
  `DEAD` during the 12-proxy check failure (07:30–08:10); each got two
  automatic resumes and has not parked since 08:02. `homelab-6` got eight

## Fixes deferred

- **Fleet capacity exceeds free slots (sixth night running).** Hosts
  `homelab` and `worker-60-14` want more containers than there are free
  accounts. **Decision for the owner:** lower those hosts' capacity by the
  surplus on the Hosts tab (stops containers, no login), or add accounts to
  the pool (those workers would then claim and log in).
- **`homelab-6` keeps dying right after every resume.** It used all eight
  automatic resumes again on 11-17, each followed by a `DEAD` park within
  1–51 minutes; every resume restores the browser session, so this is eight
  logins a day for nothing. **Decision for the owner:** check the park's
  `detail` and its proxy on the worker page and fix or move the proxy, or
  leave it parked; don't resume it manually until then.
- **Optional, carried over: tell a cut-off body from a bad one.** A
  truncated upload is answered `422 bad_request`, same as an invalid payload.
  Answering `400` with a `detail` like `body unreadable` when `c.req.json()`
  throws would separate the two in the logs. **Decision for the owner:**
  whether that is worth a contract change (worker image and fleet agent
  would have to treat the new status like `422`). Not urgent; nothing was
  lost.
